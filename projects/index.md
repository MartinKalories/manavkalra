  <h2>Electrifying Australia’s Road Freight</h2>
  <p><strong>Group Project</strong></p>
  <p>This project evaluates the feasibility of transitioning Australia's road freight industry from fossil fuels to renewable energy sources, aiming for net-zero emissions by 2050.</p>
  <h3>Key Findings</h3>
  <ul class="skills-list">
    <li>Road freight contributes 38.7 Megatonnes of CO2 annually, representing 16% of Australia's total emissions.</li>
    <li>EV trucks offer immediate abatement potential with 0 tailpipe emissions, but face challenges in range (current ~300km vs. diesel 2000+km) and charging time.</li>
    <li>By 2026, advancements promise 600km range and 30-min charging, making electrification viable.</li>
    <li>Proposed infrastructure: Government-owned land rented to private operators for charging stations, with subsidies and demand charges to ensure profitability.</li>
  </ul>
  <h3>Financial Analysis</h3>
  <p>Scenario modeling showed a realistic middle-ground scenario yielding $10 Billion NPV gain, with $8.7 Billion to companies and $1.3 Billion to government through 15% tax on post-subsidy income.</p>
  <h3>Stakeholders</h3>
  <ul class="skills-list">
    <li>Manufacturers, Electricity operators, Freight companies, Government, General public</li>
  </ul>
